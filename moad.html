Game in development

Mystery of Ancient Darkness

A dark premium 2.5D action-RPG roguelike set in 1930s Egypt: aristocratic pulp archaeology, cosmic horror, realistic cinematic movement, and a marriage tested against things older than language.

This is the current flagship game project: Lord Eugene Compton and Lady Alice Compton descend into Egyptian tombs after their son is taken through a black stone. The game is not a mascot platformer and not retro pixel art. It is a physically grounded, painterly, cinematic side-view game where every jump, stumble, shot, fall, climb, wound, and resurrection has weight.

Design Thesis

The project sits where Prince of Persia's readable movement, Darkest Dungeon's expedition pressure, and 1930s occult pulp meet. The rule is realism first: no floaty jumping, no mid-air steering tricks, no toy physics. The player survives by reading bodies, ledges, weight, fatigue, fear, ammunition, light, supplies, and the room itself.

MovementPrototype

Rotoscope-grade frame animation

The production path is hand-directed, high-resolution painted frame animation. Current proofs use 16- and 24-frame walk/run cycles, a heavy Lord Eugene walk, and accepted 8-keypose jump doctrine. AI interpolation is treated as a diagnostic helper, not a production authority.

ProtagonistsCanon locked

Lord Eugene and Lady Alice Compton

Eugene is older, larger, war-marked, and brutally protective. Alice is aristocratic, brilliant, faster, physically distinct, and increasingly tied to a dangerous African ritual power. They are not mirrored builds; their mechanics follow biography, sex, trauma, class, love, and supernatural cost.

StructureIn design

Action-RPG roguelike pressure

Rooms are explored as dangerous spaces rather than abstract tiles. Supplies, medicine, fatigue, sanity, ammunition, route choice, and the cost of pressing on matter. The London-to-Cairo journey is planned as a route-and-refuel layer before the tomb descent begins.

Occult systemsCanon locked

Transformation has a price

If Eugene dies, Alice can resurrect him automatically. The player is not asked; Alice chooses him. Each resurrection advances her transformation by one step. The early playable build tracks this with tokens rather than full transformation sprite sets, so the game can prove its structure first.

Current Production State

  • Prologue film pipeline: a 13-beat canonical prologue exists as stills and rendered video segments, with Alice's Moleskine handwriting and Eugene's Underwood typewriter as distinct voices.
  • First tomb room: a Godot greybox already tests side-view movement, ledges, a papyrus pickup trigger, and an Ancient-god reality-corruption effect.
  • Animation proof: Alice has 24-frame walk/run proofs; Eugene has a first heavy-walk candidate. The next quality gate is cleaner continuity, better centering, and repaint/layer fixes for equipment.
  • Visual floor: final room art must not drop below the premium painted 2.5D tomb target: rich stone, cinematic light, layered depth, readable platforming architecture, and no placeholder-looking final surfaces.
  • Ancient gods: the gods do not appear first as inspectable monsters. They corrupt the painted world: masonry, frescoes, cracks, shadows, camera, sprites, and UI begin lying before a body is allowed.

Production Method

The project is also a proof of a new small-studio workflow: a human art director/designer working with named AI collaborators as artists, engineers, animators, reviewers, and continuity keepers. The output is not "one prompt." It is thousands of decisions: pose, silhouette, timing, body mechanics, lighting, prop continuity, level readability, and whether the image still belongs to the same world.
